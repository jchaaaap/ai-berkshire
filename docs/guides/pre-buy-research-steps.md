# From "Interesting" to "Buy": 8 Steps Before You Buy a Stock

A screen or a headline makes a stock look interesting. These eight steps decide whether it becomes a position. Work through them in order, and stop at any step that fails.

**Worked example throughout:** LPL Financial (LPLA). It surfaced on a screen using estimated figures, which is exactly why step 1 comes first.

---

## 1. Verify the numbers

Pull the latest **10-K** (annual) and **10-Q** (quarterly) from [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) or the company's investor relations site. Confirm:

- Revenue growth
- Margins
- Free cash flow
- Debt

Never trade on figures you haven't checked. Screens and articles are often stale, adjusted or simply wrong.

> **LPL:** the screen figures were estimates. Confirm them against the filings before anything else.

Repo tools: [`/financial-data`](../../skills/financial-data.md) (two-source cross-check), [`/earnings-review`](../../skills/earnings-review.md) (primary-source read).

## 2. Understand how it makes money

Explain the business in two sentences: who pays, for what, and what makes that income rise or fall. **If you can't, stop here.**

> **LPL:** it serves independent financial advisors and earns advisory fees plus interest on client cash. That cash income falls when the Fed cuts rates.

## 3. Listen to the last earnings call

Read the transcript or listen to the recording. Note:

- What management is worried about
- What analysts push back on
- Anything that changed since the previous call

## 4. Write the bear case

List the three things that would make this a bad investment. If you can't think of any, you don't understand it yet.

> **LPL:** rate cuts shrinking cash income, advisor recruiting slowing, and integrating acquisitions.

## 5. Set your price

Estimate fair value, then set a **"buy below"** price that leaves a **20–30% margin of safety**. A great company bought at the wrong price can still be a bad investment.

Repo tools: [`/company-valuation`](../../skills/company-valuation.md), [`tools/financial_rigor.py`](../../tools/financial_rigor.py). For a quick sanity check on multiples, see the [P/E Ratio Cheat Sheet](pe-ratio-cheat-sheet.md).

## 6. Size it

| Rule | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Starter position | ~2–5% of non-retirement money |
| Adding | Only if the thesis holds up |
| Single-stock cap | Under ~10% |

## 7. Write your sell rules first

Before buying, write down what would make you sell. A **broken thesis** is a reason to sell; a price drop alone is not.

Repo tools: [`/thesis-tracker`](../../skills/thesis-tracker.md) to record and track the thesis, [`/thesis-drift`](../../skills/thesis-drift.md) to compare versions over time.

## 8. Buy in steps

Split the purchase over a few weeks, or around earnings, rather than all at once. It reduces the cost of bad timing.

---

## One-page checklist

- [ ] Numbers verified in the 10-K / 10-Q
- [ ] Business explained in two sentences
- [ ] Latest earnings call read or heard
- [ ] Three-point bear case written
- [ ] Fair value and "buy below" price set (20–30% cushion)
- [ ] Position size decided (start 2–5%, cap ~10%)
- [ ] Sell rules written down
- [ ] Purchase split into tranches
